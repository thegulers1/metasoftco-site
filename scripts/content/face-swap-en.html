What is face swap? Face swap is the use of artificial intelligence to detect a face in a photo or video and replace it with another person's or character's face while keeping the angle, lighting and expression. In this article we explain how the technology works, how it differs from deepfakes, what safe use looks like and how it is used at events.

What Does Face Swap Mean? A Short Definition

In everyday use, "face swap" covers two things: two people's faces trading places, and one person's face being placed into a ready-made scene, character or costume. The face swap you meet at events and brand activations is almost always the second: the guest's face is placed inside a visual world designed in advance.

Face swap is often confused with deepfakes. The difference lies less in the technology than in purpose and consent. Face swap is visual entertainment a person chooses, knowing the result is a transformation. A deepfake usually means content made to show someone saying or doing something they never did, often without their knowledge.

How Does AI Face Swap Work? (4 Steps)

Whatever the tool, AI face swapping goes through four basic steps:

  1. Face detection: The model finds the face or faces in the image and marks their boundaries.
  2. Landmarks: Around 68 key points, such as the corners of the eyes, the tip of the nose, the edges of the lips and the jawline, are marked within milliseconds. They describe the angle and expression of the face.
  3. Alignment and encoding: The face is aligned to the angle of the target image, and an encoder turns it into a numerical representation of the person's identity.
  4. Generation and blending: The model generates the new face in the target scene, blending skin tone, light direction and edges so the face looks like a natural part of the scene rather than pasted on.

Deep learning and CNNs

Face swap systems are built on convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Trained on large numbers of face images, these networks learn to recognise anatomical features such as eye position, jaw structure and nose shape. Face detection and landmarking are done with them.

GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks)

The first generation of realistic face swap relied on GANs. Two networks work against each other: one generates images, the other tries to tell real from generated. Over time this competition produces convincing results.

Diffusion models: Stable Diffusion, ControlNet and identity preservation

In recent years diffusion models have become the main technology behind face swapping. Models such as Stable Diffusion build an image step by step from noise. Extra layers steer that process: ControlNet holds pose, facial structure and composition steady, while approaches such as IP-Adapter and InstantID carry the identity from a reference photo into the new image. With LoRA a model can be taught a specific style, costume or product.

The difference with diffusion is that the face is not just copied into a scene; the scene is regenerated together with the face. Hair, light and clothing fit the concept as well, so the result looks less like a montage and more like a new photograph. How to Swap Faces with AI

There are three ways to do it. Which one fits depends on the quality you expect, how many people you need to serve and where the photos are processed.

With phone apps

App stores offer many face swap apps. You usually take a selfie, pick a template and get the result within seconds. It is practical for personal use. The point to watch is that the photo is often uploaded to the app's servers: check the privacy policy for how long photos are kept and whether they are used to train models.

With web tools

Browser-based tools let you upload two photos and swap the faces, or generate new images from a reference photo. Some also let you run open-source models (such as Stable Diffusion workflows) on your own computer; the photo then never leaves your device, but you need a powerful graphics card.

Is Face Swap Safe? Consent, KVKK/GDPR and Deepfake Risks

A face directly identifies a person. Whether face swap is safe therefore depends less on the technology than on how the data is handled. At events, the main points are:

  • Clear, informed participation: Guests should know their photo will be taken, how it will be transformed and where it will go. A photo should only be processed once the guest has posed and chosen a concept themselves.
  • Privacy notice and separate consents: If a form is used, the privacy notice is shown with it. Consent to marketing is collected separately and optionally, never as a condition for receiving the content.
  • Retention: How long photos are kept should be decided before the event. In MetasoftCo's Data-Capture set-ups, data is kept for 90 days by default and then deleted; a different period can be agreed in the contract. At some events photos are deleted when the event ends.
  • Children: Activations for children should run with a parent's consent and supervision.
  • Using celebrity faces: Putting a celebrity's face into campaign visuals without permission carries serious legal risk under personality rights and advertising rules. Work with a well-known person should secure the use of their likeness by contract.
  • Labelling: A watermark or frame note stating that an image was made with AI prevents it from being misread wherever it is shared.

Responsibility for the data should also be clear from the start: the client brand is the data controller for personal data collected at the event, and the technology provider processes it only on the brand's instructions.

6 Tips for a High-Quality Face Swap

  1. Lighting: Soft light from the front gives the best result. Harsh side light or backlight makes facial features harder to read.
  2. Angle: A face looking straight at the camera or at a slight angle gives the most natural result. Similarity drops in profile shots.
  3. Glasses, hats and hair: Sunglasses covering the eyes, hats covering the forehead and hair falling across the face make identity harder for the model to read.
  4. Resolution: The face must be large and sharp enough in the frame; detail is lost in distant or blurry photos.
  5. Scene choice: The closer the face angle and light in the target scene are to the photo, the more convincing the result. That is why event concepts are designed around the shooting area.
  6. Skin tone matching: A good system adapts skin tone to the scene while keeping the person's own tone. If people do not recognise themselves, the settings are wrong.
