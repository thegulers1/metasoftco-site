## Brand and art direction

### What works

- The black canvas, cyan/purple accents, condensed wordmark and mono labels create a coherent creative-technology character.
- Space Grotesk/Manrope/JetBrains Mono roles are understandable: statement, body, technical annotation.
- Real event photography and AI outputs are visually strong once they appear.
- The visual system avoids generic white SaaS cards and cheap rental-site styling.
- Repeated framing, dark panels and crisp color accents can support a premium international identity.

### What weakens premium perception

- Aurora gradients, shimmer text, glowing dots and “live” badges recur on nearly every hero. A signature becomes a template when overused.
- The first screen is predominantly copy and decoration; premium experiential studios lead with the work.
- Very long SEO titles are rendered as H1s, making services feel like search landing pages rather than designed product stories.
- The two floating conversion controls—AI ideas and WhatsApp—read as competing widgets and obscure content.
- Many detail pages use a narrow blog/article column even when the subject demands spatial, physical and visual proof.
- The red AI-chat UI is visually disconnected from the site’s cyan/purple system.

Recommendation: keep the dark, precise visual identity; reduce decorative repetition; make footage, outputs, installations, client marks and people the primary color/motion system.

### Hero findings

The current H1—“Markanı etkinliğin kahramanı yap.”—is memorable but not categorical. The paragraph names Stable Diffusion and ControlNet before explaining the business result. “30 Günde Sahnede” looks like a benefit but behaves as a contact CTA; if 30-day delivery is not universally guaranteed, qualify it.

The metrics `1K+`, `100+`, `5+`, and `∞` mix verifiable evidence with a decorative infinity. Replace infinity with a useful, verifiable operating fact or remove it. The existing metrics should link or annotate methodology if used as enterprise proof.

### Recommended homepage sequence

1. **Positioning + moving proof:** clear category, outcome, audience, primary CTA, 10–20s muted showreel/poster.
2. **Verified client proof:** selected logos with “selected collaborations,” avoiding an unqualified trust claim if work was subcontracted.
3. **Featured activation stories:** three cases, each with objective, experience, technology and one verified result/fact.
4. **Capabilities:** AI experiences; photo/video; interactive games; installations; custom software/data.
5. **How MetasoftCo delivers:** concept, design/prototype, production, on-site operation, reporting.
6. **Business outcomes:** branded UGC, footfall/engagement, lead capture/consent, product storytelling, participation.
7. **Technology and production reliability:** in-house team, throughput, fallback, moderation, hardware, deployment markets—only verified facts.
8. **Use cases:** launches/brand activations, conferences/trade shows, retail, corporate events.
9. **International capability:** markets, languages, partner model and logistics evidence.
10. **Event brief CTA:** date/location/audience/objective.

## Service listing

Strengths: filtering is understandable; cards show actual outputs; category breadth is visible.

Issues:

- 36 services create choice overload and a product-rental impression.
- The page mixes commercial capability groups with speculative/empty categories.
- Cards do not communicate buyer context, guest flow, format, lead capture or proof.
- Category hubs are not consistently linked, contributing to orphaned SEO hubs.
- English listing emits a literal `/null` URL when translation data is incomplete.

Recommendation: lead with five capabilities, show 2–4 proven formats within each, then offer “Explore all formats.” Add card fields: best for, guest output, footprint/format, customization, linked case.

## Service detail pages

### Current template

Breadcrumb → category badge → SEO-title H1 → description → video → rich text → specs → FAQ → gallery → generic contact/related services → generic CTA.

### Problems

- H1 frequently contains “Rental/Kirala,” category and brand suffix, creating search-copy visual noise.
- No compact “what the guest does” explanation appears early.
- Technology is described more often than event value and operating constraints.
- Real project proof is not structurally linked into the template.
- Important procurement questions are inconsistent: throughput, footprint, staffing, power/internet, print/share options, accessibility, moderation, data retention, setup time, countries.
- English pages can show Turkish specs/FAQ because localized fields are not selected in the shared client.
- Narrow `840px` pages make physical activations feel like articles.

### Recommended template

1. Short service name + outcome-led subhead + strong real visual/video.
2. “Best for” contexts and 3–5 verified operating facts.
3. Guest journey: approach → capture/input → AI/interaction → output/share/print.
4. Brand customization: interface, prompts/styles, physical skin, output, email/landing page.
5. Production/technical: footprint, power/internet, throughput range, staff, accessibility, contingency.
6. Data and measurement: consent, captured fields, analytics/reporting, retention.
7. Real case proof.
8. Gallery showing installation, participant, interface, input/output and branded deliverable.
9. Native-language FAQs.
10. Related formats and a prefilled event brief.

## Project/case-study UX

The project listing has strong brand names and visuals. It should become the principal sales tool.

Current case pages lead with a long title, paragraph, client/category panel and imagery. They often do not distinguish challenge, idea, implementation and result. On mobile the breadcrumb/title can clip and the floating CTA obscures content.

Recommended case structure:

- one-sentence outcome/idea
- client, event/campaign, location, date, duration, activation type
- challenge/objective
- creative idea
- guest journey diagram or four steps
- technology and production scope
- physical setup + branded output + people using it
- verified results/operating facts
- client quote only if approved
- deliverables/team/partners, accurately attributed
- related capability and contextual “Build a similar activation” CTA

## About and trust

The About page is visually clean but too brief for an international buyer. It states founding context and technical ambition without showing the team, process, production environment, support model or geographic capability.

Add:

- clear company definition and legal/business identity
- leadership/team with roles and relevant expertise
- “how we work” from brief to post-event report
- workshop/prototyping/production imagery
- verified history and scale methodology
- markets served and partner/crew model
- data/privacy and AI moderation principles
- operational reliability: testing, backup, staffing, escalation
- selected clients/projects with correct relationship attribution

## Contact and conversion

### Current strengths

- Direct phone, email, address, WhatsApp and form offer multiple paths.
- Form fields are short enough for a basic enquiry.
- service selector/quote panel can support high-intent users.

### Friction

- “Contact,” “Get a Quote,” AI event ideas, service selector and WhatsApp compete without one primary conversion model.
- Generic name/email/phone/subject/message does not gather enough scoping information for a useful first response.
- No expectation is set for reply time, process, budget fit, geographic availability or confidentiality.
- Source page/context should be captured automatically.
- Form privacy consent/notice and data handling are not prominent.
- Floating controls cover mobile form/content.

### Recommended CTA system

| Context | Primary CTA | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Plan Your Activation / Etkinliğini Planla | View Case Studies |
| Service | Brief This Activation / Bu Aktivasyonu Planla | See a Real Project |
| Project | Build a Similar Experience | Explore the Capability |
| Insights | Discuss Your Event Brief | Read Related Case |
| About | Start a Project | See Our Work |

Event brief fields: name, work email, company/agency, event date, city/country, expected attendance, objective, selected format, required output/data, message. Use progressive disclosure; do not make every field mandatory. Show expected response time only if operationally true.

Track: CTA impression/click, form start, validation error, form submit, WhatsApp click, email/phone click, service selected, project-to-brief conversion, language and source route. Avoid duplicate GTM/gtag firing.

## Mobile audit

### Confirmed at 320/375/390/430/768

- status/pill labels extend beyond the viewport and are clipped by global `overflow-x:hidden`.
- secondary hero CTA clips on narrow widths.
- metric rows retain spacing/width that cuts labels and values.
- long service/project titles overflow or clip rather than reflow comfortably.
- breadcrumbs are single-line and cut off.
- persistent AI CTA is partly off-screen at some widths and covers text/media at others.
- WhatsApp and AI controls compete for the same right-bottom safe area.
- the navigation shell is visually empty on mobile except logo; hamburger capture timing/state should be retested because some screenshots showed only the logo.
- card/media views preserve desktop density instead of prioritizing one decisive proof asset.

### Fix principles

- remove the global “hide the symptom” overflow rule after identifying each source.
- use `min-width:0`, `max-width:100%`, `overflow-wrap:anywhere` selectively, and multi-line breadcrumb truncation.
- set mobile H1 by content role, not one viewport clamp; aim ~34–44px for long service names.
- stack or horizontally scroll metrics intentionally with complete labels.
- reduce persistent CTA to one compact control; expose WhatsApp inside the contact sheet/drawer.
- respect safe areas: `env(safe-area-inset-*)`.
- make media full-bleed where valuable and text columns comfortably padded at 320px.
- test touch targets ≥44 CSS px, focus, landscape and zoom at 200%.

## Typography and design system

Eight Google font families are globally declared: Inter Tight, DM Sans, Lato, Barlow Condensed, Poppins, Space Grotesk, Manrope and JetBrains Mono. The captured experience mainly needs three.

Recommended lightweight tokens:

```text
Font display: Space Grotesk 600/700
Font text: Manrope 400/500/600
Font label: JetBrains Mono 500 (sparingly)

Type: 12 label / 14 meta / 16 body / 18 lead / 24 h3 / 36 h2 / 56–76 hero desktop
Mobile: 12 / 14 / 16 / 18 / 24–28 / 36–46
Space: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72, 96
Radius: 10 control / 16 card / 24 feature media / 999 pill
Container: 1240 wide / 840 article / 760 reading
Color: canvas, raised, border, text-1, text-2, text-3-AA, cyan accent, violet accent, success, error
Motion: 120/200/400ms; no continuous motion under reduced-motion
```

Do not standardize away the expressive identity. Standardize the invisible rules—spacing, text contrast, control sizes, media ratios, motion behavior—so project art can vary without the interface feeling inconsistent.

## Imagery and video plan

Each flagship capability needs this six-shot proof set:

1. wide physical installation in venue context
2. participant actively using it
3. close-up of interface/input
4. generated/branded output at full quality
5. delivery/share/print moment
6. crowd or brand-team outcome

For AI transformations, show input → generation → final branded asset. For games, show action, scoreboard and spectator engagement. For custom installations, show fabrication/prototyping and on-site technicians. Avoid generic AI art that could have been produced without the event.

Video strategy: one fast homepage showreel; 15–30s muted case clips with poster; captions; no autoplay audio; responsive sources; viewport loading below the fold; actual `VideoObject` only when the video is visible and metadata is valid.

## CRO experiments after P0/P1 fixes

1. Hero: outcome-led positioning + showreel versus current abstract hero.
2. Primary CTA: “Plan Your Activation” versus “Get Event Ideas.”
3. Service proof: case card immediately after guest journey versus near bottom.
4. Brief: short contact form versus progressive event brief.
5. Case cards: result/fact overlay versus category/date metadata.

Define one primary success metric—qualified submitted briefs—and guardrails for form completion, spam, response time and lead quality. Do not A/B test while 500s and mobile clipping remain.
